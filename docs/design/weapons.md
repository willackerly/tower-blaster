# Weapons: blasts, triggers and scripted munitions

Status 2026-11-01. Full write-up with the reasoning and the alternatives: the "Grenades and Scripted Weapons" artifact linked from the PR that added this file. Weapons are built and tried in the viewer first (it is becoming a superset of the game); Keystone's par and leverage rules are not a constraint for now.

## What exists

**`BlastEvent`** (`packages/engine-api/src/blast.ts`): an input event that pushes every body whose centre lies within `radius` of `centre`, away from it, at the start of the step it is due:

```
|dv| = min(maxDv, impulse * w(d) * A / m)
w(d) = 1 inside core * radius, then ((radius - d) / (radius - core * radius))^falloff   (integer falloff)
A    = a quarter of the body's surface (its mean silhouette): 2 (hx hy + hy hz + hz hx) for a box, pi r^2 for a sphere
```

- `centre` is a point, or `{ body, offset? }`: that body's position when the blast is due, read from the engine's live state (a pipelined reader that sees the grenade one step late still blasts where it is). The body must exist, and not be removed, when the blast is due; `checkInputQueue` refuses the event at `init`/`push` otherwise.
- After each blast's kick a pushed body's speed is capped at `maxSpeed` (default 48, 0.8 of the sweep limit at 60 Hz; scaled with direction kept; `Infinity` turns it off), so stacked kicks cannot tunnel. Units are the engine's (cube units in a `scale`d world, where the sweep limit is larger still).
- Applied in event order with the spawns of the same step; a blast pushes only the bodies that exist when it is due.
- The kick is added to linVel and to the adaptive warm start's previous velocity (reference `prevLinVel`, GPU `SolverBody.prevVel`), so it is not read as an acceleration.
- Only +, -, *, / and one square root, so the f64 reference is the same on every machine.
- Implemented by the reference, the GPU engine (`packages/physics-gpu/src/blast.ts`: one dispatch on steps that have blasts, none otherwise) and Rapier. `TieredEngine` refuses blasts until it wakes dormant cells in the radius (`wakeSphere` exists).
- Tests: `@tb/testkit`'s `blast.ts` is one shared case (bodies in free flight around the centre, a spawn before and after the blast, an excluded id, and a second blast in the same step centred on a moving body with an offset and a speed cap); every engine must change exactly the velocities `blastKick` gives and leave every other body bit-identical (reference to 1e-12, Rapier and GPU to 1e-5 relative). The GPU also blasts a 6 x 4 x 6 block of cubes against the reference.

**`RemoveEvent`** (`packages/engine-api/src/remove.ts`): `{ step, kind: 'remove', id }` takes a body out of the world at the start of the step it is due, in event order with that step's spawns and blasts (so a grenade's blast and then its removal, at the same step, push the bodies around it and then take it away).

- The slot is a tombstone: ids are stable and never reused (the next spawn gets the next id; a GPU engine's capacity counts removed bodies). The body has no pairs and no ground contact, feels no gravity, blast or solve, and stays exactly where it was removed with zero velocity. Snapshots report it with `tier` `TIER_FREE` (255, the renderer's `TIER.free`, which every pass skips) and `awake` 0; `Snapshot.tier` is present once any body has been removed.
- Refused at `init`/`push` (`checkInputQueue`, before anything changes): a removal of a body that does not exist when due or is already removed, and a blast centred on a removed body. A blast never pushes a removed body.
- Reference (`BodyState.removed`): the broadphase runs over live bodies only, the ground manifold and the inertial target skip it. GPU: the host zeroes its velocities and sets its flags to `TIER.free << 8`; every kernel guards on that tier (prepare writes a negative bounding radius, which the broadphase skips; narrowphase writes an empty ground manifold; initBodies freezes it; the blast kernel skips it). Rapier: velocities zeroed, then `setEnabled(false)`. `TieredEngine` refuses removals, as it refuses blasts.
- Tests: `@tb/testkit`'s `remove.ts`. A free-flight case (a spawn, a blast, then two removals at one step, including the body spawned that step; a later removal, then a blast centred on a live body that reaches two removed ones): removed bodies are frozen where they were removed (reference 1e-15, GPU and Rapier 1e-6, quaternion renormalisation), at zero speed, tier free from the next snapshot on; every other body bit-identical to the run without removals on all three engines. A stack case: the cube under another is removed, and the top one falls to the ground while the one beside it does not move.

**Relative spawns** (`packages/engine-api/src/spawn.ts`, plan step 5): a `SpawnEvent` with `from: { body, axes? }` places the new body in a parent's frame, from the parent's state in the engine rather than from a prediction of it. `body.pos`, `linVel` and `angVel` are then vectors in the frame's axes; the child takes the parent's whole rigid motion:

```
r = F pos      world pos = P + r      linVel = V + w x r + F linVel      angVel = w + F angVel      rot = q rot ('body') or rot
```

- Axes `F`: `world` (identity: offsets as in the world, moved to the parent), `body` (the parent's rotation: a pattern fixed to the shell, turning with it), `heading` (local +y along the parent's velocity, straight up below 1e-6 units/s; local x level and across the heading in flight; a rotation). A spinning shell's fragments fly off along the rim as well as on its heading.
- When: the parent's state **as the step starts**, before any event of that step, whatever order the events were pushed in. So "spawn the children from P, then remove P" at one step is a split, and a blast at that step does not change what a child inherits; a blast pushed after the child at that step pushes it, one pushed before does not (event order, as for any spawn). The parent must exist at the start of the step (in the scene or spawned at an earlier step) and not have been removed at an earlier step; `checkInputQueue` refuses the event otherwise (and, given the engine's `stepIndex`, treats events due at past steps as sharing the next step's start).
- Children of one mass whose offsets and own velocities sum to zero carry the parent's momentum on exactly (the centre of mass goes on with the parent's velocity, spin or no spin): `@tb/weapons`' `conserve`.
- Reference and Rapier read the parents before applying the step's events (`relativeBody`, in f64 on the reference). The GPU engine writes the child's slot from its description as for any spawn, then a one-thread-per-spawn pass (`packages/physics-gpu/src/spawn.ts`) at the very start of the step's command buffer reads the parent's `Body` and writes the child's world pose and motion, `SolverBody.prevVel` and, with sleep, its quiet window's anchor. For that read to see the parent as the step starts, removals are now copies of a tombstone (linVel, flags, angVel) inside the step's command buffer after that pass, instead of queue writes, which would land before any of the step's work; nothing else moves (the solver's pinned digests are unchanged). `TieredEngine` refuses relative spawns.
- Tests: `@tb/testkit`'s `spawn.ts`. A free-flight case (a blast pushes a parent before its child spawns; children in world, body and heading axes, one from a parent at rest; a split, the parent removed at the same step; a blast after the spawns pushes two children; a child of a child three steps later): one step after each spawn, the child is where `relativeBody` of its parent's state in the run's own snapshot, the later blasts and one step of free flight put it (reference 1e-12, Rapier and GPU 2e-5 in f32, with and without sleep), and every scene body is bit-identical to the run without the spawns. A burst case: a spinning cube of mass 8 into 8 children of mass 1 on centred offsets and velocities keeps its momentum to 1e-14 on the reference and 1e-6 in f32.
- Alternatives weighed (2026-11-06). *Parent read in event order* (a blast before the spawn at the same step changes what the child inherits): more expressive, but it makes the result depend on push order, and the GPU would need the blast pass split around each spawn; the step-start read is one pass and the same on every engine. *Predicted parent state on the host* (what splits did before, `flightPathFrom` free-flight lookahead): no engine change, but it is wrong as soon as the parent touches anything between the read and the split, and late by the reader's lag; it is the bug this step removes. *Spawn with world state computed on the GPU from a host template* (no `from` in the contract): hides the semantics in one engine, untestable on the others. *Recoil on the parent and `emit` (children over several steps)*: deferred; a burst that conserves momentum covers the weapons asked for, and emit is a rule (`every`) of spawns, already expressible.

**Forces** (`packages/engine-api/src/force.ts`, plan step 6): a `ForceEvent` `{ step, kind: 'force', id, force }` gives body `id` a force for `force.steps` steps from the step it applies, replacing any it had; `force: null` ends it, and so does the body's removal. Three kinds, every number in the world's units:

- `thrust: [x, y, z]` N, `axes` `world` (default), `body` (turns with the body) or `heading` (+y along its velocity: a rocket's motor);
- `vibrate: [x, y, z]` N, `period` s, `phase` (fraction of a period at its first step): `F = vibrate sin(2 pi (k h / period + phase))`, the sine by the fixed-series `cosSin`, so every machine shakes alike;
- `pull: [x, y, z]` (a world point), `stiffness` N/m, `damping` N s/m, `max` N: a spring and a dashpot, `F = stiffness (pull - x) - damping v`, its length capped. A held drag: the game re-pushes it as the hand moves.

On each step a body has a force, after every other event of that step (spawns, removals, blasts), its velocity changes by `dv = h F / m`, with F from its state then; the kick goes onto the adaptive warm start's previous velocity too, as a blast's does, so a force is not read as falling. That is a force over the step for the solver (the inertial target moves by `h^2 F / m`). At most `MAX_FORCED` = 64 bodies carry a force at any step; `checkInputQueue` refuses more, a force on a body that does not exist or is removed then, and a malformed force (`checkForce`). Engines keep a `ForceTable` (what force events set) in their checkpoints. The reference and Rapier apply `forceKicks` (Rapier wakes the body each step). The GPU engine's force pass (`packages/physics-gpu/src/force.ts`) runs after the blast pass in the step's first compute pass, one thread per forced body: world thrusts and vibrations come up from the host as world vectors (`forceIsWorld`), body and heading thrusts and pulls are evaluated in f32 from the body's own state. It sets the awake bit, so a sleeper is woken with its group by the wake pass as a blasted one is, and `SleepBody.forced` (the former pad word) keeps the body from falling asleep at the end of a step it is forced; a forced step is never skipped (`sleepSkip`). `TieredEngine` refuses forces.

- Tests: `@tb/testkit`'s `force.ts`. A free-flight case (all five kinds of force at once, a pull that reads its body's state after a blast at the same step, a spawn and its thrust in one step, a force replaced, one ended by null, one by the body's removal): every step of every body is one step of free flight from the engine's own state before it, after the step's blasts and its kick (reference 1e-12, Rapier and GPU 2e-5 in f32, GPU with and without sleep); a never-forced body is bit-identical to the run without forces; two GPU runs are bit-identical. The held pull: a sleeping column of three cubes, the middle one dragged 3 units out: the whole column wakes the next step, the top cube falls, the dragged cube stays awake while held, then is pressed still onto the ground and still stays awake until let go, then sleeps (with `SleepBody.forced` removed the GPU test fails at the press). Rapier and the GPU replay a checkpoint taken while forces act bit for bit.
- Alternatives weighed (2026-11-06). *Forces folded into the inertial target as an acceleration in the solve* (the plan's first sketch): the same motion in free flight, but it needs a per-body force buffer read by prepare and every solver variant; the kick at the start of the step is one tiny pass, and the inertial target already moves by `h^2 F / m` through the velocity. *A per-body force field in `Body`* (dense, every body): 16 bytes x 100k bodies to carry what 64 bodies use. *Forces as repeated blast events* (the host pushes a kick every step): no contract change, but it ties the host to the step rate, a frame's lag makes a held pull jerky, and a heading thrust needs the velocity the host has not read yet. *Torques and off-centre forces*: deferred; nothing asked for spins a body up. *Pull as a weapon action*: no; a pull is the player's hand (a game primitive); weapons get `thrust` and `vibrate`.

**Grenades** (`packages/weapons/src/grenade.ts`, `@tb/weapons`): pure logic over snapshots. The caller fires the grenade's spawn, tells `Grenades.launch` about it and feeds `Grenades.observe` the states it reads; both return the events to push: the blast (centred on the grenade, excluding it), then the grenade's removal at the same step (`detonation`). The grenade is spent: it vanishes when it goes off.

- Fuses: `timer` (due `delay` s after the spawn, known at launch, no observation needed), `prox` (the first step whose state has the grenade within `prox` of another body's surface or the ground, after `arm` s), `proxTimer` (`delay` s after that).
- Proximity is decided from observed state k and is due at k + 1 at the soonest, so a pipelined reader lands it at the same engine step as a serial one. `observe` looks `lookahead` steps ahead along the grenade's exact free-flight path (`freeFlight`), with the other bodies where state k has them, and a trigger predicted for step j is due at j. So a reader observing every step with lookahead 1 and one observing every 4 steps with lookahead >= 4 detonate at the same step, bit for bit, while nothing near the path moves much (`packages/weapons/test/grenade.test.ts`, on the reference engine).
- Distance is exact to spheres and oriented boxes, with the grenade taken as its bounding sphere. A coarse cull against the bounding sphere of the predicted path keeps the cost at one distance per body for the far ones.
- `DEFAULT_GRENADE` is deliberately strong: radius 5, impulse 40 per m^2, flat core of 0.2 radius, so a 1 kg scene cube within 3 m gets the full 25 m/s.

**`WeaponSpec`** (`packages/weapons/src/spec.ts`, `runtime.ts`): a weapon as data. A shell (an engine-api shape, density, friction, restitution and, for the root, a launch speed range), rules of the form `when` trigger -> `do` actions, and named child munitions that splits make, nested at most 3 deep (root, children, grandchildren) with no references back up. Every number is in the world's units; `scaleWeapon` converts lengths, speeds, density and blast strength together.

```json
{ "version": 1, "name": "firework",
  "shell": { "shape": { "kind": "sphere", "radius": 0.4 }, "density": 4, "speed": [20, 35] },
  "rules": [{ "when": { "any": [{ "apex": true }, { "after": 3 }] },
              "do": [{ "split": { "into": 8, "child": "star", "pattern": "cone", "angle": 40, "spread": 6, "at": 0.9 } }] }],
  "children": { "star": {
    "shell": { "shape": { "kind": "sphere", "radius": 0.2 }, "density": 4 },
    "rules": [{ "when": { "near": 0.05, "arm": 0.2 }, "do": [{ "blast": { "radius": 1.5, "impulse": 6 } }, { "split": { "into": 4, "child": "spark", "pattern": "ring", "spread": 3 } }] },
              { "when": { "after": 4 }, "do": [{ "remove": true }] }],
    "children": { "spark": { "shell": { "shape": { "kind": "sphere", "radius": 0.05 }, "density": 2 },
                             "rules": [{ "when": { "after": 1 }, "do": [{ "blast": { "radius": 0.5, "impulse": 1, "falloff": 2 } }, { "remove": true }] }] } } } } }
```

- Triggers: `after` s (from the spawn, or from when the first of a `then` held), `near` m with `arm` s and `ground` (Grenades' proximity fuse), `apex` (vertical speed <= 0), `then [a, b]`, `any [...]`. Actions: `blast` (engine-api Blast fields, centred on the munition plus `offset`, not pushing it), `remove`, `split` (`into` children of a named child on a `ring`, `sphere`, `cone` or `fan` pattern at `at` from the centre, with `spread` outward; the munition is removed). Since plan step 5 a split's children are relative spawns (`SpawnEvent.from`) of the munition, pushed the step the rule fires (no lookahead): `ring` and `sphere` in world axes, `cone` and `fan` (a level fan of `angle` either side of the heading) in heading axes, so the engine places them from the munition's real state. `conserve: true` centres the pattern (subtracts the mean direction from every child's), so offsets and spreads sum to zero: with children weighing what the munition weighed (the `mass` warning flags a spec whose children are more than 1% off) the burst carries the munition's momentum on exactly. A rule fires once; its actions apply in order at its step; an action after `remove` or `split` in the same rule is refused.
- Not yet: `contact` with a speed, `remote` (a recorded tap), `duplicate`, `emit`, `stick`, `vibrate`, `thrust` (plan steps 5 to 7).
- `validateWeapon` returns issues `{ code, level, path, message }`. Errors refuse the spec: `json`, `version`, `name` (weapon and child names match `/^[a-z][a-z0-9]*$/`, so a rack item `w-<name>` is safe in shot text), `key` (unknown key), `type`, `range`, `shape`, `trigger`, `action`, `order`, `child` (a split names no child), `depth`, `budget` (over 256 bodies), `crowded` (a split's children would start overlapping). Warnings: `unused-child`, `never-spent`. `writeWeapon` is canonical (fixed key order), and `parseWeapon(writeWeapon(s))` is `s`.
- `weaponBudget`: the static worst case, every rule of every munition firing once: bodies (what an engine's capacity must hold for a shot), depth, each munition's count and each blast's count, so a rack can charge `launch KE + sum count * Y(blast)` (spectacle-mode.md section 4; Y is the rack's).
- `WeaponRuntime` runs specs as `Grenades` runs grenades: `launch(id, step, weapon, shape)` and `observe(snapshot, step, shapes, idOf?)` return input events. Triggers are decided from observed states with the same lookahead (an open trigger cannot fire up to the lookahead), a rule's events go out once no rule that spends the munition could fire first, and split children start from the munition's predicted state at the split step. `idOf` numbers a child's spawn by the engine's order. A `GrenadeSpec` runs as `grenadeAsSpec`, and the spec'd grenade replays bit for bit as the built-in one on the reference, for every fuse and reader cadence (`packages/weapons/test/runtime.test.ts`). Children are placed from free-flight prediction, so at another reader cadence they start alike to rounding, not bit for bit; step 5's engine-side relative spawn removes that.
- Split patterns use only +, -, *, / and square roots (`cosSin` is a fixed Taylor series), so every machine places children identically.

**Real things to throw** (`packages/weapons/src/projectiles.ts`, `PROJECTILE_PRESETS`): a beach ball, a basketball, a bowling ball, a cannonball, a wrecking ball and the Raiders of the Lost Ark boulder, each at its real diameter, density, restitution, friction and a launch speed that suits it, with a natural colour and a candy skin. The viewer fires one with `proj=<name>` (the panel's "throw" picker, which fills in the thing's own fields; any can be overridden with the usual `proj.*` keys, and a saved scene's `projectile.preset`).

- Units: a real thing stays real whatever the cube size. Its radius and speed are divided by `scale` (m per cube), so under real gravity (9.81/scale cube units/s^2) it flies as it would. Its density is set against `blockDensity`, the real density a scene block of density 1 stands for: stone (2400 kg/m^3) by default, pine (450) for the Kapla tower, and a Keystone level's stone (density 625) for real stone. Mass over a block cube's mass (blockDensity x scale^3) is what the solver sees, and the panel shows it ("0.249 kg, 1/9,650 of a 1 m cube of block (2.40 t)"). `proj.blockDensity` (the panel's "blocks are" field, with stone, brick, oak, pine, cardboard and foam suggested) says otherwise: lighter blocks let lighter things move them. Plain `ball` and `cube` keep their cube-unit, froudeSpeed semantics.
- A real thing's launch speed is capped at `launchSpeedCap`: a physics step's travel stays under 0.8 of what the AVBD broadphase looks ahead (`maxSweep`, 1 unit per 1/(60 substeps) s) plus half the thing's radius, so it is at most half its radius into a block it was not seen to reach and its centre never passes a face. It binds only in small worlds: of the six, only the cannonball in the Kapla tower (2.34 cm units, 7 substeps), at 19.6 m/s instead of 45. The panel says when it binds. Ball and cube leave as set, as they always did (the default scene's 80 m/s ball included).
- Bounce: a thing's own restitution needs the engine's restitution switch (Bounce); with Bounce on, it keeps its own e instead of the scene's (the engines average the two).
- Candy is a skin, not physics: the renderer's per-body fx gains a `paint` bit (`FX_PAINT`), so the thing is drawn its own colour (its candy skin in the candy and hazy looks, its natural colour in the real look) rather than the look's ball colour.
- Checked on the reference engine (`packages/viewer/test/projectiles.test.ts`): a beach ball, 1/9,650 of a stone cube, comes off a 2 x 2 x 3 tower without moving a block or a NaN; a wrecking ball moves more than a third of it and the boulder all of it.

**Energy: one unit, grenades** (`packages/weapons/src/yield.ts`). A blast's nominal yield (`nominalYield`: the kinetic energy it would give a packed fill of one kind of block at rest, the same number on every machine, within 3% of a real lattice of cubes) is what the rack charges. One **grenade** is `GRENADE_YIELD`, `DEFAULT_GRENADE`'s yield in the viewer's world (`CUBE_FILL`: packed 1 kg cubes of edge 1). `weaponYield(spec, fill)` sums every blast a spec can set off (`weaponBudget` counts) and `grenadeEnergies(spec)` divides by the unit. A weapon written in the viewer's cube units scales to a level (`cubeScale`) with all its yields scaled by one factor, so its grenades are the same on every level, plaster or stone (`keystone-lab/test/rack-weapons.test.ts` checks B1 to B3 to 1e-9).

| Weapon | Grenades | Note |
|---|---|---|
| viewer grenade (`DEFAULT_GRENADE`), `GRENADE_WEAPON`, B1 to B3's `bomb` | 1 | the unit |
| `firework` (12 stars, radius 3, impulse 18.5) | 1.004 | 0.389 before 2026-11-02 (radius 2.5, impulse 15) |
| `cluster` (6 bomblets, radius 3, impulse 30) | 0.994 | 0.800 before (impulse 25) |
| `grenades`, `grenadem`, `grenadel`, `grenadexl` (`GRENADE_SIZES`: the viewer grenade with its radius 3.15, 5, 7.94, 12.6; same kick, so it reaches further rather than harder) | 0.25, 1, 4, 16 | rack `w-grenades` .. `w-grenadexl`, viewer `proj.weapon=grenadel`; a bare `w-grenade` is `w-grenadem` (since 2026-11-05) |
| `KEYSTONE_GRENADE` (w-grenade's default until 2026-11-05; SI, not scaled with the block; a level may still write it into its `weapons`) | 0.013 on 0.5 m plaster, 0.002 on 0.5 m stone | the "firework costs 30 grenades" figure was against this one |

On a level's page in the viewer's sandbox (`?level=B1&proj.weapon=grenade`, or `firework`, `cluster`, a `weapon.def`), cube units stand for the level's commonest block too (`inLevelGrenade`, `inLevelWeapon` with `cubeScale`), so the page's grenade is the level's bomb, blast for blast, and a 31 kg plaster block in its core gets 17.7 m/s (25 sqrt(0.5)) rather than the 0.48 m/s an unscaled one gave it.

**`w-grenade` is `w-grenadem`** (Will, 2026-11-05: "weapons should always behave the same, and if you want smaller bombs just call them smaller"). A bare `w-grenade` with no `weapons.grenade` of the level's own is the M grenade, one grenade scaled to the level's blocks like every other built-in; the small one is `w-grenades`. Alternatives weighed: keeping `KEYSTONE_GRENADE` (1/77 of a grenade on plaster, 1/479 on stone, so its strength changed with the level's material: the opposite of the rule); defaulting to B1 to B3's `bomb` (the same blast in a bigger, faster shell, but a level's own weapon, not part of the shared S/M/L/XL vocabulary); dropping the bare name (breaks shot text and the any-scene-as-level default rack). No shipped or cast level defaults it (their hashes are pinned in `keystone/test/rack.test.ts`); the any-scene levels (`AUTO_LEVEL`, stars uncast) now fire the M grenade. Its shell is a WeaponSpec shell, so in the game it has no impact sound, like the firework's and the bombs' (`game/src/sound-map.ts` gives sounds to Keystone's iron and steel ammo only). `KEYSTONE_GRENADE` stays for a level that writes it in, and as the GrenadeSpec the keystone-lab tests fire.

So a rack of built-ins is a choice of how the same energy is spread (one big blast, 12 small ones over a cone, 6 medium ones where they land), not of how much there is. Retuning a built-in is safe for cast levels: a w- item the level does not define plays the built-in, which is part of the level hash, so stars cast against the old one drop.

## Known limits of the v1 blast

| Limit | Consequence | Fix (planned step) |
|---|---|---|
| the speed cap can slow a body | a body already faster than `maxSpeed` inside the radius leaves slower than it came | intended (it guards the sweep); `maxSpeed: Infinity` for scenes that want none |
| pair pool is 16 per body | the default grenade inside the default S1 tower needs 13.5 per body (`packages/physics-gpu/test/pair-room.test.ts`); stronger knobs or faster packed blocks can need more, and then the viewer's HUD says so in red with a Rebuild button | done: drops are loud everywhere (docs/plan.md, GPU pair buffer); GPU blast tests fail on any dropped pair |
| no occlusion | a body behind another is pushed as hard | none planned |

## Plan (each step lands with its tests)

1. **Blast event** (done, #90).
2. **Grenade in the viewer**. 2a (done): body-centred blasts and the final-speed cap in all three engines. 2b-i (done): fuses as pure logic in `@tb/weapons`. 2b-ii (done): `proj.weapon=grenade` in the viewer's sandbox, with timer and proximity fuses, blast centred on the grenade (`centre: { body }`), configurable from the Scene panel, the URL (`grenade.*`) and the scene file (`weapons.grenade`). The page reads the state about once a frame and the fuse predicts 4 steps of 1/60 s ahead (times substeps). Under Keystone's rules a level's rack names its items (`packages/keystone/src/rack.ts`, roadmap G3): Keystone's ammo bare, a preset as `p-<preset>` (real size and bounce, density at the model scale, x 625/2400), a weapon as `w-<key>` (the level's `weapons[key]`; a GrenadeSpec has lengths in h. `w-grenade` defaulted to `KEYSTONE_GRENADE` until 2026-11-05, since `DEFAULT_GRENADE` in SI gives a 31 kg plaster cube only 0.48 m/s; it is now `w-grenadem`, the viewer grenade in cube units scaled to the level, so a weapon behaves the same on every level and a smaller one has a smaller name). Since G3.3 the Driver runs the rack's weapons itself (`packages/keystone/src/arsenal.ts`, on `WeaponRuntime`), so the game, its replay, `runSession` and the bot push the same events; a blast is charged its nominal yield (`nominalYield`, `packages/weapons/src/yield.ts`). Since G3.4 a level's `weapons[key]` may be a `WeaponSpec` in the viewer's cube units (what Download weapon writes), and `w-firework`, `w-cluster` are the built-ins: `cubeScale` maps cube units onto the level's commonest block (lengths x h, speeds x sqrt(h), densities x the block's, impulse so the block gets the cube's Froude-scaled kick), and its whole budget counts for capacity and the GPU's room.
