# Agent playtest round 1 (G9b)

Status 2026-11-06. Will is away and delegated the calls; this round stands in for the human tester round (G9) until he is back. It plays every level and mode on main at desktop and phone sizes, has independent Opus judges review what it captured, and turns what they found into ranked roadmap rows.

## Result in one paragraph

All 107 runs played to the end with no page error (desktop, phone and phone held sideways; every rated level, all 30 trials, both Siege Tables, Bullet Time, three looks). Three independent Opus judges raised 72 issues between them. They agree on what already works: the gold-crosshair crown and teal-shield heritage language on the chamber levels, the aim preview with its blast dome, the scorecard-style result card, the 10,000-block collapse, and the Bullet Time HUD flow. What most holds the game back is not the physics but **what the player can see and when**: objectives hidden behind other blocks on the city levels, sparks that bury the collapse, a phone HUD that covers half the screen, result cards that land on the payoff, a Siege Table camera that centres your own king, and long waits after shots that did nothing. The ranked list below folds the 72 into 19 roadmap rows (`PT1` to `PT19`), each with its evidence and a verify.

## How the round ran

`pnpm playtest:agent` (`packages/playtest/src/agent/`): a Vite server on the game page as checked out, headless Chromium, and for each scenario of `plan.ts` a fresh browser context at the scenario's viewport.

| Viewport | Size (CSS px) | Device pixels | Input |
|---|---|---|---|
| desktop | 1280 x 800 | 1x | mouse |
| phone | 390 x 844 | 2x | touch (`isMobile`, `hasTouch`) |
| phone-landscape | 844 x 390 | 2x | touch |

The plan (unit-tested in `packages/playtest/test/agent-plan.test.ts` so coverage is not a hope):

- **Front door** at all three viewports: as it opens, How to play, Advanced settings, the Trials list.
- **Every rated level** (B1-B3, L1-L5 with their lot sheets, U1-U3) played as a first visit at desktop and phone; the recorded line (par, or B1/B2's showcase) on the desktop where one exists. L3 also exercises Undo and Retry.
- **Every trial** (30, including the ice trials T-I1-T-I3) played on the desktop, its aim view on the phone; the ice trials again in the real look.
- **Bullet Time** on B1 (3 freezes, desktop and phone) and L5 (2 freezes): fire, freeze 30 steps in, capture the frozen world, fire into it, settle.
- **Siege Table** D1 and D2 at desktop and phone, four turns.
- **Looks**: L3 and B2 in the real and candy looks (hazy is the default everywhere else).
- **Phone held sideways**: B1.

Each run writes its captures (the aim view, the first shot at 20, 60 and 150 sim steps, every settle, the result card, undo and retry), a contact sheet, and what it measured: each shot's sim time from fire to settle, collapse, strokes, the result (stars, total, stamp, card text), HUD text, lot-sheet state, undo mode and time, and every page error or warning. Round 1 was 107 runs, 562 captures and 70 contact sheets, just under an hour with three workers on a 4-core cloud box.

### What the harness cannot see, and how it compensates

- **No canvas presentation here.** Headless Chromium on SwiftShader cannot present WebGPU to a page canvas (`Could not find SharedImageBackingFactory ... WebgpuSwapChainTexture`; the device is lost on the first frame, with or without Xvfb and every ANGLE/Vulkan flag tried). So the game page gained `?offscreen=WxH` (params.ts `offscreenOf`, tested): it renders offscreen at the viewport's own pixel size, and before each screenshot the harness draws that frame into a 2D canvas laid over the game's canvas, under the HUD. The image is the game's own frame at the viewport's shape, with the real DOM HUD on top.
- **Physics per scenario.** The chamber levels, Undercut, the duels and L3's looks run on the GPU solver, the players' engine. The 1,000 to 10,000 block levels and the trials run on Rapier, because the GPU solver on SwiftShader takes minutes a shot there. Two consequences. The cast levels (B1, B2) show "custom: stars not recorded" in this round, because stars cast on one engine are not applied on another (`onEngine`), and their outcomes differ (B1's showcase line scores one star here, three on the M5). And Rapier lets a resting structure sleep, which this round found ends a far shot at step 20 with the ball in the air (PT10); the first pass ran the chamber levels on Rapier and was discarded for that reason. Claims that could be Rapier's alone were re-run on the GPU solver (`gpu-check/` in the project files): T-S8 stands there, T-X4 does not, and T-I3's counters behave.
- **Picture and HUD from different steps (found after the round, PT12).** A capture drew the scene at the step it was asked for, then the screenshot read the HUD; on Rapier the sim kept stepping on the CPU while SwiftShader took many frames to draw, so a "+25 steps" frame showed the scene at step 25 under a HUD some 150 steps on. Headless, at step 25 B1 reads Down 0% on Rapier, and T-I3 reads 0% and 0/9 on both Rapier and the f64 reference (`keystone-lab/test/counters-engines.test.ts`); at step 200 Rapier reads 17% and 85% with 9/9, the frames' numbers. So PT12's counters were right. The runner now holds physics (`GameHandle.hold`) from before the picture until after the screenshot, and names each frame by the step it held at. Past the first impact Rapier and AVBD do part (T-I3 at step 60: D 0.17 on Rapier, 0.05 on the reference): that is the engines' collisions, not the counters, and is why the rated levels play on the GPU solver.
- **Times are sim steps.** Every time in this report is in sim steps (60 per second), which is what a player waits through at real pace on a machine that keeps up. Wall times on SwiftShader are never performance results (CLAUDE.md); frame pacing and load wall time stay with the Mac bench queue.
- **No sound.** Headless audio is muted; sound has its own thread and judge (A-series rows).

### Alternatives considered

**How to play.**
1. *Scripted bot lines only* (the par and showcase lines, the cast bot's intents): deterministic and competent, but they play the level as an expert does and never see what a newcomer sees (the opener, a miss, an empty rack, a bank). Used for the levels that have a line, as the "competent player" half.
2. *Exploratory agent play* (a model looks at each frame and decides where to click): closest to a person, but one decision per screenshot at a few seconds a SwiftShader frame makes a level take tens of minutes and the run is not repeatable. Rejected for round 1; the judges get the images instead, which is where a model's eyes add most.
3. *A seeded novice policy* (chosen, with 1): the HUD's pre-aimed opener when offered, then clicks near the middle of the screen with a small seeded jitter, the first reachable spot of a centre-out spiral when a click must land on a block (Undercut's pulls). It is what a first visit does with no strategy, it is repeatable run to run, and its misses are as informative as its hits.

**How to judge.**
1. *One judge with every image*: one perspective, but about 100 contact sheets is more than one review holds well.
2. *Scored rubric only* (numbers per level): comparable across rounds, but numbers without the why do not make roadmap rows.
3. *Three Opus judges by area, one shared rubric* (chosen): core loop (door and rated levels), modes (Bullet Time, Siege Table, Undercut, phone and touch), and breadth (trials, looks, ice). Each gets the contact sheets, the measured numbers and the harness caveats above, but not this thread's own notes, and returns issues with severity, evidence (file names) and a proposed fix. Findings that two judges or the measurements agree on rank higher.

The rubric each judge used: first-30-seconds clarity (what to hit, what to spare, how to fire); feedback and impact (cause and effect readable, a shot feels like it did something); pacing (waits between shots, when the result card comes, idle time); HUD and layout (legible, not covering the action, at both sizes); looks (composition, lighting, materials, consistency between looks); mode-specific clarity; bugs and glitches. Severity: P0 broken or blocking, P1 hurts most players, P2 polish, P3 nice to have.

pnpm playtest:agent --only '^L3-' --workers 1
