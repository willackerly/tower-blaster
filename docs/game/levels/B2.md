# B2 Spare the Cathedral

The second big level of the spectacle slice (roadmap G5.3, [spectacle-mode.md](../spectacle-mode.md) section 11). Play it at `?level=B2` (game), or play on from B1.

## Settings

| | |
|---|---|
| Structure | 5,184 plaster cubes: S14 town, 25 towers 4 x 4 cubes and up to 16 high (heights varied 40%), streets 4, seed 3 (`B2_TOWN`) |
| Crowns | the middle 4 cubes of the tallest tower's top course (`cathedralLevel(..., 'middle')`) |
| Heritage | the middle tower's top two courses: the cathedral, 32 cubes, -500 score each when broken |
| Rack | a bomb and two rams (`BOMB`, `RAM`) |
| Objective (★) | every crown down, footprint at most 0.05 |
| Stars metric | **score** (the tally): crowns down is 4 for every star, so it cannot separate them |
| Physics | the game's defaults: GPU solver, 40 iterations, settle v3 |
| Level hash | `c75fbd9832e6814b` (the stars apply only while B2 hashes this; `castStars`) |

How it was chosen (Metal explorations, drafts B2a to B2e in `keystone-lab/src/drafts.ts`, 16 competent plays each): with the whole 16-cube roof as crowns, competent play aimed anywhere downed them in 0 to 6% of plays and broke a median 24 of the cathedral's 32 cubes. A competent player aims at what the objective names, so the bot now aims at the crown tower (#144). Aimed there, the whole roof fell in 13% and the middle 4 in 31%, with the cathedral intact in the median play. A third ram or a drop changed neither, so B2 is the middle 4 with a bomb and two rams.

A play's value is its best bank point (`endValue`): the best met settle if it met the objective, else its last settle.

**What the thresholds mean for a player.** Under these stars the seed-1 plays earn:

| Plays | none | ★ | ★★ | ★★★ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| competent (64) | 49 | 2 | 6 | 7 |
| nudges of the best line (50) | 2 | 0 | 8 | 40 |

So on B2 the objective itself is the hard part (a competent play meets it 23% of the time). One star is the crowns down. Two stars is the crowns down without wrecking the cathedral: the 15 competent plays that met the objective scored 2,349 to 3,805, except two that broke most of the cathedral and scored -12,904 and -12,040, which keep one star. Three stars is a clean, economical demolition close to the best line (3,200 of a best seen of 3,805): crowns down, the cathedral intact, and rack left over. Of the nudge cloud, 40 earn three stars, 8 bank 3,028 to 3,176 with the cathedral intact (two stars, just short of T3), and 2 miss a crown. Ten nudges broke some heritage on a later shot, but each had met the objective first with the cathedral intact, and banking then is worth more.

**T2 is noisy, T3 is not.** T2 sits in the gap between failed plays (negative, from heritage broken) and met ones (over 2,300), because 77% of competent plays never meet the objective. A bootstrap of the competent median puts its 90% interval at about 333 to 1,110, but every value in that interval falls in the same gap, so the ★★ line separates the same plays wherever it lands in it. T3's half-width is 67. Seed 2 (the recast) is queued on the Mac at low priority; when it lands, the two seeds are pooled (`pnpm keystone:cast B2 --pool B2-s1.json,B2-s2.json`), as for B1. Alternatives considered: wait for seed 2 before shipping stars (it crashed once and is a 100-minute job on a shared Mac), or define T2 as the median of the competent plays that met the objective (2,915 here; it would make ★★ mean "a good clean demolition", but it changes the spec's rule for every level, so it is a question for the spec, not for B2).

The best line (shot text, `readShots`), B2's showcase (`solutions.ace`): `w-bomb.500.8.155.173.79_w-ram.711.8.273.259.95_w-ram.528.8.216.481.80` (climbed from `w-bomb.500.8.155.171.84_w-ram.711.8.273.279.85_w-ram.528.8.216.481.80`).

## Proofs

- **Compile and stars apply (CI):** `keystone/test/big-levels.test.ts`: B2 is 5,184 cubes with 4 crowns and 32 heritage cubes, its stars carry its hash, the showcase uses B2's rack in order, and moving the crowns to the whole roof drops the stars.
- **Rest and showcase (Metal, or `PHYSICS_GPU_SLOW=1`):** `keystone-lab/test/big-levels-gpu.test.ts` runs B2's pre-settle and rest, and replays `solutions.ace` and checks it meets the objective with no limit hit. At 5k, SwiftShader's pre-settle alone takes about 10 min, so these run on the Mac, not in a CI shard.

## The lost cast (seed 2) and the fix

Seed 2 stopped at play 136 of 186 with `page.evaluate: Execution context was destroyed`, while the Mac was in use. The cast page's JavaScript context went away under a batch, and with it the whole run. Nothing in the cast page reloads itself, and Vite runs without watching or HMR, so the trigger was outside the cast (the cause is not known). The cast player now recovers (`keystone-lab/src/page-retry.ts`): when a batch fails because the page, its target, its renderer or its GPU device went away, it opens a fresh cast page on the same browser, checks that it reports the same adapter, and replays the whole batch, up to twice per batch. That is safe because each play runs from the level's start on a fresh engine, so a replayed batch returns exactly the same plays (tested on SwiftShader with the page navigated away and with it closed mid-batch: `cast-gpu.test.ts`). Any other error still stops the cast. A recovered cast lists each reopen in its markdown and JSON (`reopens`). Alternative not taken yet: checkpoint the plays to disk so a cast whose browser dies can resume; the retry covers a lost page, not a lost browser.

## The recast (seed 2): a search failure, not a level change

Seed 2 ran on the M5 at a406519 (with the reopen fix; no page was lost) and failed fair (0.54), skill, stable and budget; the recast gate failed with |T3 seed 1 - T3 seed 2| = 7,721. Its competent plays match seed 1's (objective met 16 of 64 against 15, median 721.5 against 773.5, same hash), so the level and its physics did not change. What differed is the line the climb chose:

- The climb judges each candidate by the lower quartile of only 4 nudged plays, and on B2 a play's value is bimodal (crowns down, about 3,000; not, negative). A line meeting the objective about 55% of the time scores all 4 met with probability 0.55^4 = 9%.
- Seed 2's best line was the same base line judged four times (three of its eight moves changed nothing the shots show: a clamped or rounded step), and each time it got fresh nudges; the climb kept its luckiest sample (4 of 4 met, score 2,855.5). Over all 66 nudged plays of that line (16 in the climb, 50 in the cloud) it met the objective 38 times: 58%.
- Seed 1 happened to start a climb in a robust basin (its cloud met the objective 48 of 50 times, every one on the bomb).

Seed 2's 227.7 min was the Mac, not the level: 30.1 ms per engine step against 15.3 for seed 1 (453,808 steps against 406,768, only 12% more), with Will at the machine.

**Fixes (cast.ts):** a line judged again pools its samples; a move that changes nothing is drawn again; and before the cloud the climb's 3 best distinct lines get 8 more nudges each and the best by its pooled sample becomes the best line (`confirmLines`, `confirmNudges` in the recipe: the short recipe is now 210 plays). On a simulated level where a fifth of lines meet the objective 95% of the time and the rest 50%, the old climb picked a fragile line in 12 of 60 casts and the new one in 4 (rarer robust lines, a tenth: 22 and 14); confirming 5 lines, 16 nudges or 2 x 12 did no better, so the remaining misses are casts whose climb never reached a robust line. Pooling now refuses a cast that failed fair, skill, limits or complete (the seed 1 + 2 pool printed T3 1,600 and exited 0), and judges fair, skill and stable again on the pool; `--stars-into` writes only when those pass.

B2's stars stay at seed 1's. Next: a 16-nudge check of seed 1's line on current main (`--cloud-of`), then a fresh recast (seed 3, new climb) to pool with seed 1.

## Recasting

Any change to B2's town, crowns, heritage, rack, weapons, physics or settle rule changes its hash and drops the stars. To recast on the Mac: `/mnt/project-files/g5/cast-b2.sh` (two seeds, about 100 min each), then pool and land the stars in one step: `pnpm keystone:cast B2 --pool B2-s1.json,B2-s2.json --stars-into packages/keystone/src/stars.json` (it writes B2's line in `stars.json`, which the level reads through `castOf`; pool only casts whose gates passed).
