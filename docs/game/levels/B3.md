# B3 Ten Thousand

The third big level of the spectacle slice (roadmap G5.4, [spectacle-mode.md](../spectacle-mode.md) section 11), and the slice's one true 10k level. Play it at `?level=B3` (game), or play on from B2.

## Settings

| | |
|---|---|
| Structure | 10,000 stone cubes: a city of towers 5 x 5 x 25 with streets 3 (draft B3d) |
| Rack | three bombs (`BOMB`) |
| Objective (★) | collapse at least 0.4 |
| Stars metric | collapse |
| Physics | the game's defaults: GPU solver, 40 iterations, settle v3 |
| Level hash | `f11e947a868b602f` |

How it was chosen (Metal explorations B3a to B3e, spectacle-mode.md section 11): the third item (a wrecking ball or a ram) added nothing after two bombs; the skyline stayed 1.0 in every play (some tower always stands), so B3 is judged on collapse; 20 iterations bought nothing over 40. A play is about 1 min on the M5 (20 ms a step), so a short-recipe cast is about 3 to 3.5 h against the 60 min budget gate, raised for this level (`--budget-min`).

## What the two casts say

- **The climbs found different lines.** Seed 2's line is good in its median (0.69) but has a long lower tail (P10 0.40), so its T3 is low. Seed 1's line is the better one by T3. The recast gate assumes two seeds find the same basin; on B3 (and B2) they do not, so it fails for a reason that says nothing about the stars' noise.
- **Skill pays little on B3.** Pooling every competent play (128) gives T2 0.535; seed 1's line gives T3 0.634; the gap is 0.099 against the gate's 0.100. Three bombs into a 10k stone city do roughly the same damage wherever they land: aiming the best line known brings down about 10% more of the city than a typical attempt.
- **Stable is sampling noise of seed 1's cloud**, which more nudges of the same line fix (`--extend`; the interval narrows as 1/sqrt(n): 100 nudges, about 0.037 expected).

The stars file records the waiver (`"waived": ["skill"]`). Only skill and stable can be waived (`WAIVABLE` in `cast.ts`); fair, limits and complete say whether the best line meets the objective and whether the plays are the game's physics, so they never are.

**The skill margin got thinner with more evidence, not thicker.** The extension lowered seed 1's T3 from 0.634 (50 nudges) to 0.617 (100), so against the pooled T2 the gap is 0.082, not the 0.099 the call was first made on. Seed 1's extended cast alone passes every gate (its own T2 0.516 gives skill 0.101), but that is the T2 estimate that happens to pass; the pooled T2 (128 plays) is the better estimate, so the stars use it and the shortfall is stated rather than avoided. The difference for a player is small (★★ at 51% or 53% down).

**What the thresholds mean for a player:**

| Plays | none | ★ | ★★ | ★★★ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| competent, both seeds (128) | 20 | 43 | 33 | 32 |
| nudges of seed 1's line (100) | 1 | 2 | 21 | 76 |
| nudges of seed 2's line (50) | 5 | 8 | 5 | 32 |

Three stars comes to a competent player about a quarter of the time and to a player near the best line about three times in four. That is the thin skill margin in player terms: on a 10k stone city, three bombs do roughly the same damage wherever they land.

**Why waive rather than redesign or recast.** A redesign that makes aim pay (a load path, cores or lintels the X-ray can show; fewer, heavier items) is the spec's own mitigation for this risk (spectacle-mode.md, risks), but it is a new level and another 3 to 7 h of casting. Recasting does not help: the two seeds found different lines, and a third seed adds another line rather than evidence about seed 1's. The redesign stays open as roadmap row B3r, to be decided by playtest question (k) (is 10k better than 1k?) and whether players feel their aim mattered.
