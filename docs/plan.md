# Engine plan

Full report with research, measurements and sources: https://claude.ai/artifact/5ZzMkLSGUwCSunVGMMU8yK

## Summary

- **Targets.** 10k cubes all moving at 30 fps (p99) minimum; 100k stretch. Target machine: Apple M5 Max.
- **Physics.** GPU-only. CPU engines (Rapier measured at about 11 µs per active cube per step, single-threaded WASM) are references, not candidates.
- **World model.** Four tiers: lattice (intact towers, near-zero cost), active rigid (full solver), frozen rubble, debris particles. Loose towers first (exact column-support rule); glued towers as an option later.
- **Solver bake-off.** AVBD (primary), GPU Soft Step, mass-splitting Jacobi, substepped XPBD, all on shared broadphase, narrowphase, persistence and sleeping.
- **Harness first.** Scenarios S1 to S11, quality gates, interleaved A/B with bootstrap confidence intervals.

## Loaded-gap slop (AVBD reference)

Opt-in and off by default (`AvbdParams.gapSlop`, metres; off when absent or 0, and then the reference is bit-identical to before). The GPU engine applies the same rule in f32 (a pipeline constant: off, its pipelines are built as without it and, on SwiftShader, give the same floats; the point's flag is a bit of `SolverContact.used`, so contacts stay 128 B; with manifold records, `AvbdParams.records`, the GPU default since 2026-10-27, a bit of the record's `flags` or `stick` word). Full description: the header of `packages/physics-ref/src/avbd.ts`.

- **What it fixes (defect B).** Post-stabilization lifts a body out of its start-of-step penetration without velocity and leaves its other loaded points a few um in the air; the next main iterations counted that gap in full and dropped the body back with velocity, so rocking stacks gained energy every step and never came to rest (a sideways-kicked 8-cube column at 10 iterations, kicked packed towers). With `gapSlop`, a point still loaded from last step, not a rolling contact, counts its start gap less `gapSlop` in the main iterations. At 3e-5 m those cases come to rest or keep a bounded sub-mm sway.
- **Why it is off.** It is a fixed length, measured on unit cubes at 1/60 s: a plank on a narrow pivot and 0.1 m cubes need 1e-4 or more, while S13's rocking test is at its bounds by 5e-5, and at 10 iterations 3e-5 already puts S13 at its bound once the start is re-rolled (3 of 12 draws below). On Keystone it settles more shots but L2's real-rest check fails more often (table). Round 3 of the defect B work is evaluating scale-free versions (the length taken from the contact manifold or from the warm λ/k); the default waits for its verdict.
- **On the GPU** (SwiftShader, 2026-10-27; `engine-gapslop.test.ts` and `engine-gapslop-column.test.ts`). The kicked column comes to rest as on the reference, and an excused gap stops closing a few um up in f32 (`solver.ts`). With 3e-5 injected into the GPU and reference sides of engine-stacks, engine-rest and engine-knock, all pass, with two shifts to weigh before a default: S13 2 deg keeps less margin (far side 1.164 deg against 1.192, 0.592 against 0.606 of the rigid swing, with 0.58 the backstop; the reference moves alike, 1.242 to 1.207 deg), and the S5 knock's every-step deepest penetration has a heavier tail (speeds 13-17, each unmoved and re-rolled 4 times, so 25 runs per arm: median 36 mm either way, over 55 mm in 9 runs on against 1 off, worst 99.6 against 79.0 mm; the reference 6 on, 8 off; the cut was chosen after looking, paired sign test p 0.42). Re-measure that with 40+ runs at Keystone's 20-40 iterations before it is on there. On the M5 Max (Metal, Chrome, 2026-10-27, at 8d5fffb) it builds with the default dispatch and costs nothing measurable: the S4 10k city at 10 iterations, throughput mode, 3.419 ms/step median with it on against 3.450 off (-0.84%, [-0.98, -0.76]; under the bench's 2% threshold). `engine-gapslop.test.ts`'s bit-exact on/off and cross-form checks were tuned on SwiftShader and have not run on Metal (vitest on the Mac runs SwiftShader).
- **Keystone's first par shots** (reference, f64; each run's start moved by up to 1e-6 m; the settle rule of `rules.ts` on main, spin counted on the structure only; 2026-10-27). "Settled" counts runs under the 600-step cap; "real rest fails" counts settles where a body still moves by position more than its velocity says; "late v" is the median over runs of the structure's largest speed late in the run.

## Fused dual (removed)

`AvbdParams.dualFusion` (perf-competition round 2) ran each contact's dual update at its first visit in the next primal sweep instead of in a pass between sweeps: the same arithmetic on the same values, so bit-identical to the separate pass on the reference and on SwiftShader, with 2 (I - 1) fewer dual dispatches per step. Its state lived in a side array beside the read-only contacts, or in place with `GpuEngineOptions.solveContactsRW`. It was removed after the M5 Max sweep of 2026-10-27 (`pnpm bench:probe` P2 on the S4 10k city, Metal, at 10 and 20 iterations, steps 60 and 300):

- **Side array.** The primal's cost per contact point rose 41-43% (median over the 8 direct colours; rule P allows 5%), the tail's per critical-path point 26-27%. The solve pass got slower, not faster: about 1.9 ms per step more at 20 iterations (14.4 to 16.2 ms at step 60) and 0.8 ms more at 10, where the bar was I x 60 µs less (1.2 and 0.6 ms).
- **In place.** Per point +19-20%; the solve pass 0.3-0.4 ms shorter, below the same bar.
- **On records 1 or the lattice colouring** (against the same variant unfused) the per-point rise was 40-50%.

The dispatches it saved cost less than the work it added to every body's walk. Since the removal, giving `dualFusion` or `solveContactsRW` to either AVBD engine throws, so an old bench spec cannot run the default under its name. Every remaining option gives the same floats as before the removal on SwiftShader (`engine-pinned.test.ts`), and the shaders differ only in two struct fields renamed back to padding (`SolverBody.visit`, `ColorParams.fuse`). `pnpm bench:probe-compare` now reports a base and a variant side by side, with rule P's checks listed, and judges nothing.

## Lattice colouring (GPU default)

`AvbdParams.colorMode` 3 (perf-competition round 2, "latticecolor"; rule in `packages/physics-ref/src/coloring.ts`) is the GPU engine's colouring when the param is unset (`gpuColorMode()` in `packages/physics-gpu/src/engine.ts`), on top of manifold records 1. A cube on the world lattice prefers the colour 2 (x + z) + y mod 2 when no higher-priority neighbour holds it; anything else colours as before. A resting city of cubes then takes 4 colours instead of 9-11. The reference engine is unchanged (unset is 0 there), and `avbd-gpu:colorMode=0` names the hash colouring alone, the GPU default before (its `EngineInfo.variant` lists `colorMode` whenever it is given).

- **Speed (M5 Max, 2026-10-27 sweep, S4 10k at I = 20).** With records 1 the step falls from 9.6 to 5.2 ms at steps 60-240 and from 5.0 to 3.1 ms at 300-480. Every P2 per-point rule holds.
- **Quality (r3-colormode, 2026-10-28).** At the game's setting (40 iterations, gapSlop 3e-5) and at 20 iterations, on the f64 reference (which runs the same rule) and the GPU on SwiftShader, against colorMode 0 and against colorMode 0 with another 1e-6 m start jitter. No gate is worse beyond colorMode 0's own spread over body orders and jitters:
  - Rest: Q5 drift falls 10-100x and sag 10-20%; an 8x8x25 city tower is intact at step 720 with peak speeds 10-40x lower, and so is an 8x8x30 tower at step 600 (GPU, 20 iterations).
  - KNOCKT: no early collapse in any order at 40 iterations. At 20 there is none in 20 orders, against 4 for colorMode 0. Penetration spikes are debris events whose rate is colorMode 0's.
  - S12 passes, and Q6 and PUSH stay within 0.02 s.
  - Keystone: every par line L1-L5 clears on the GPU at par with no heritage broken, as it does on the reference with colorMode 3.
- **Mode 4 not chosen.** It is mode 3 for the lattice interior only. After an impact it colours almost as colorMode 0 does, so it keeps colorMode 0's KNOCKT collapses at 20 iterations (6 of 19 orders) and its post-impact speed (S5).

## Restitution (AVBD)

Opt-in and off by default (`AvbdParams.restitution`); off, the reference is bit-identical to before. The GPU engine applies the same pass in f32 (`packages/physics-gpu/src/restitution.ts`, whose header has the kernels and the f32 departures). It reads points or exact manifold records (`AvbdParams.records` 0, or 1, the GPU default) through one view of either, and on SwiftShader the two give the same floats and restitution stats every step (`packages/physics-gpu/test/engine-restitution-records.test.ts`). Centre-friction records (2) refuse it. On SwiftShader its drops rebound at the reference's ratio to 1.5e-6 (2-10 m/s, e 0.3-0.9, sphere, flat and edge), and its threshold (>=), combine rules (a NaN e bounces nothing, as on the reference), depth-1 supports, mass splitting, start-of-step inertia and lambda reset match the reference's cases (`packages/physics-gpu/test/engine-restitution.test.ts`). Off, it allocates no buffer, builds no pipeline, encodes no extra dispatch and gives the same floats as before the port. On, it costs 11 + 2 × `restitutionRounds` dispatches a step (27 at 8 rounds, 4 of them the energy cap's; when nothing bounces, all but 3 of them are indirect with 0 workgroups), (maxPairs + capacity) × 96 B and 352 B per body (64 B of it the cap's). Keystone keeps it off. Full description: the header and `restitute()` in `packages/physics-ref/src/avbd.ts`.

- **Rule.** A velocity pass after post-stabilization. A contact point that carried force and approached at `restitutionThreshold` (1 m/s, compared with >=) or faster at the start of the step is raised to the normal velocity -e vn0. The points of a manifold are solved together (a centroid line search, then one Gauss-Seidel sweep), and manifolds are coupled by mass splitting over `restitutionRounds` (8) rounds, with the other loaded contacts of their bodies as one-sided supports (depth 1). A bounced point that will separate starts the next step with no force.
- **Coefficients.** e = `combineRestitution(SceneDesc.restitutionCombine, e_a, e_b)`, clamped to [0, 1]. The rule defaults to average (`DEFAULT_RESTITUTION_COMBINE`, Rapier's), in every engine that applies restitution: Rapier, and the reference with `AvbdParams.restitution` on. The ground's e is `SceneDesc.ground.restitution` (default 0), so under average a body bounces off the ground at half its e.
- **Known limits** (10 iterations; e is the pair's; under the default average unless a rule is named):
  - The bounce is step-based: measured at contact, a drop's rebound falls short of e by 2-7% at 5-10 m/s, and at 2 m/s keeps 0.76 of e at e 0.3 and 0.62 at e 0.15 (an e 0.3 body on the default ground).
  - Simultaneous impacts in a chain of touching bodies (bounded by the energy cap since E4b; `restitutionEnergyCap`, default on). Without the cap the pass's converged answer can gain there (the simultaneous Newton law's flaw: from one GPU knock's state, S5 at e 0.9 and 15 m/s, 8 rounds take the struck bodies from 630 J after the solve to 729 J, 128 rounds to 957 J, 566 J at the start of the step; that knock gained 3.9% of |E0|), and a struck body that anchors hold almost entirely could take a whole bounce on its few free directions (on the reference an S5 knock at e 0.6 and 8 m/s threw two seated cubes sideways at 50 m/s, adding 2,822 J, 73% of |E0|, in one step; the jam guard, E4c below, holds such rows). With the cap no island (bodies joined by the step's contacts) ends the pass with more of the invariant energy than at the start of the step or after the solve: on the reference over 60 random struck clusters at e 0.3-0.9 (5 break it without the cap) and on 18 knocks at 10 iterations (one gains 73% without), on the GPU on all 24 S5 knocks (one gains 3.9% without), checked island by island with the host's energies. What it does not bound is the solve's own gain when it is unconverged (from that knock's state, with restitution off, 40 J in a step at 10 iterations, 180 J at 4, none at 40). Its cost is strictness: an island the solve left above its start gets no bounce that step (alpha 0), and a large island shares one budget, so a gaining corner of it scales down all of it.
  - e = 1 is not energy-safe: tilted rods gained about 0.8 of their energy, and with the ground at e 1 (rule max) tilted cubes up to 16% and tumbling ones 3.1%. A pair e of 0.95 gained 1.5% once. Beyond 8 rounds the pass overshoots at e = 1 (+0.3% at 32-64 rounds where 8 gains nothing).
  - `restitutionThreshold` 0 rests an undisturbed stack but not a struck one. A cube landing at 3 m/s on an 8-cube column (e 0.5 on every surface and the ground) left the column moving: on the reference 0.088 J and 8.9 mm of side drift 50 s into the run (0.057 m/s at 20-25 s; the GPU 0.21 m/s there). Thresholds of 0.01 and 0.1 m/s rest it to rounding. `gapSlop` (3e-5) shrinks it (reference 1.7e-4 m/s, GPU 0.023) without removing it.
  - The impulse is along n only: a cube landing on an edge at mu 0.6 keeps 0.36-0.83 of the rigid rebound and spins at 1.66-3.06x the rigid spin (rule max).
  - Heavy on light (fixed by anchors, E4; `restitutionAnchors`, default on). Without them, the supports held a struck body only as far as the rounds converge, which is slower the heavier the striker: a ball at 10 m/s on a 1 kg cube on the ground rebounded at 0.27-0.32 of e v at 5 kg and 0.02-0.04 at 50 kg, drove the cube into the ground at 4.7-5.7 m/s (0.09 off), and from 2000 kg could go through it. With anchors the ground, and resting bodies a struck body sits on, hold it exactly (the Schur complement of its loaded rows), so the striker's mass no longer matters: 0.89, 0.88, 0.84, 0.83 and 0.83 of v at 1, 5, 50, 510 and 2000 kg (e 0.9), the cube no faster down than with the switch off, a 510 kg ball 0.032 m into the cube (off 0.110), and a 144 kg rubber ball at 20 m/s 0.034 m into the top of a 10-cube column (off 0.105, without anchors 0.129) while bouncing at 0.52 of v (rule max). The GPU matches the reference to 4e-4 of v. Numbers and the rule: the reference's header and `restitute()`, "Anchors".
- **Energy cap (E4b, 2026-11-06): analysis of alternatives.** Problem: the pass's simultaneous solve can add energy where several touching bodies are struck in one step (Known limits). Requirement: a bound that holds on both engines, keeps the pass's floats where it does not bind, and is deterministic on the GPU.
  - More rounds or iterations: rejected. The gain is the converged law's (957 J at 128 rounds against 729 J at 8), not the rounds' error.
  - A cap per manifold or per body: rejected. Energy legitimately moves between manifolds and bodies through shared bodies, so a local cap either forbids that or misses the gain; measured per manifold: 729 J to 655 J, still above the 630 J the solve left.
  - One cap for the whole scene: rejected. One island's dissipation would pay for another's gain, and one bad island would scale every bounce in the scene.
  - A jam guard alone (a looser anchor tolerance, ANCHOR_TOL 0.05 instead of 1e-4, fixes the 2,822 J knock; 1e-2 does not): it treats one mechanism and changes which rows anchors hold; the cap bounds that knock too. Kept as a possible follow-up if the cap's alpha 0 steps show in play.
  - Another impact law (Poisson, or sequential impulses ordered by approach speed as in sequential-impulse engines; energetic laws after Stronge): a rewrite of the pass. Sequential order is a serial dependency, and to be deterministic on the GPU it would need a sort; energetic laws need per-contact compression and restitution phases. Not pursued while a bound suffices.
  - Chosen, a per-island cap: islands are the connected components of the step's contacts (the ground joins nothing). The pass's change to the velocities is linear in its impulses, so scaling them by alpha scales the change exactly, and an island's energy after the pass is E_ps + alpha G + alpha^2 Q / 2 (G = du.M u', Q = du.M du at the end pose); alpha is the largest in [0, 1] keeping that within max(E_start, E_ps) (`capAlpha`, closed form). At alpha 1 the cap only reads, so a step it does not scale has the floats of the pass without it. GPU: islands by lock-free union-find (compare-exchange hooking the larger root under the smaller, compressed to the least body, as sleep.ts does), sums in fixed point with integer atomics (D rounded down, G and Q up), so labels and alphas replay bit for bit; 4 dispatches (all indirect: none run when nothing bounces) and 64 B per body. On the GPU the cap scales the same step as the reference by an alpha within 3e-5 of it (two cubes into a box at e 1).
  - Reversibility: `restitutionEnergyCap: false` is the pass as it was, to the float on the reference (its tests pinned) and on SwiftShader (4 scenes against main at f45f639). Restitution off is untouched; Bounce stays opt-in, and level physics stay pinned by their hash. With the cap the spectacle limit of e 0.6 or below for Bounce (set until E4b) is lifted; the design range stays e 0.3 to 0.5 (spectacle-mode.md, Q2).
- **Jam guard and the cap's range (E4c, 2026-11-06): analysis of alternatives.** Problem: S5 at 10k with Bounce 0.5 blew up (SwiftShader, cloud and the Mac's; the Mac's two probes logged as Metal were SwiftShader, as `openWebGPU` ignored `TB_CHANNEL`). Per-step logs (`s5-blowup-probe.test.ts`) put both blow-ups in the restitution pass: at step 490 (cloud) it threw a seated cube from 3 to 43 m/s, which the cap allowed because its 6,274-body island had dissipated 1,300 J that step; at step 592 (Mac) it threw a resting ground cube to about 220 km/s, the cap scaled it only to 2,498 m/s (alpha 0.011, 3.4 times the island's bound: the fixed-point sums clamp a term at 2^29 J, which rounds Q down), and the scene ran away from there (844 m/s, 8,190 fast bodies, pair overflow). The mechanism is the anchors' jam: a row the anchors hold all but a fraction f of reaches its target only by moving the body 1/f times as fast as a free row would, up to 10^4 times at the old cut (the basis's tolerance, 1e-4).
  - A tighter cap (smaller islands, e.g. the pass's own manifolds): rejected. Unsound: the solve moves energy along every contact, so energy the solve passed to a neighbour outside the pass's set would pay for the pass twice.
  - A per-body speed or energy clamp after the pass: rejected, as in E4b (energy legitimately moves to bodies that had none: a struck cube in a row).
  - Raising ANCHOR_TOL (the basis and the cut together; 0.05 fixed the 2,822 J knock): rejected in favour of a separate cut. It changes which rows join the held basis too, where the jam is about rows outside it.
  - Friction in the pass (hold a seated body's tangential rows when Coulomb allows): the physically right cure for the remaining squirts, but a pass with friction cones is a rewrite. Not now.
  - Chosen: (1) `restitutionAnchorCut` (default 0.2, both engines): a row with under that fraction of it outside the held directions is held, so no row moves a seated body more than 5 times as fast as a free row would. 0.2 because friction (mu 0.6) would hold strikes within 31 degrees of a support's normal (a linear row's free fraction up to 0.51); the cut stops short as f mixes in rotation. Measured on the reference: the 73% knock gains nothing; in 600 small scenes (1-3 cube columns, ball mass ratio 1-50 at any aim, e 0.6) bodies sped past (1 + e) v by the pass: 21 at 1e-4 and 1e-2, 16 at 0.1, 10 at 0.2, 8 at 0.3, 5 at 0.5 (the rest are frictionless squirts at 2-4 v, not jams); the reference's tests pass at 0.1-0.3 (one pin moved by 6e-6, its old value kept under cut 1e-4). On the GPU the cut also leaves each side's cut rows out of the update's sums (`RestSlot.linA`, 80 to 96 B), else the update projected them anyway (29.8 m/s where the reference held the cube). (2) The cap's range: a term outside the fixed point (or not finite), or an addition that wraps a sum (atomicAdd's old value), flags the island and its alpha is 0, the solve's velocities; without the check the clamped sums let a 3e6 kg ball's whole bounce through (Q about 7e9 J).
  - Result on S5 at 10k, Bounce 0.5 (SwiftShader, cloud, 800 steps, through the onset window): with the guard no body passes 28 m/s, with the cap on or off, and the pairs peak at 105,037 of 160,016 (the same as Bounce off). Without the guard the same run with the cap off reached 509 m/s by step 660. Metal is still to run (the probe, now that it reaches Metal).
  - Reversibility: `restitutionAnchorCut: 1e-4` is the pass before the guard, to the float on the reference (pinned); the range check changes nothing inside the range (|island sums| under 2^29 J). Bounce stays opt-in; restitution off is untouched.
- **Energy-check exception.** The energy check the pass was landed with (gate G6 of the restitution review, not a phase gate: kinetic or shadow energy never above its start by more than 1e-9 relative, over drops, oblique impacts, stack drops and zero-g head-on pairs) fails on zero-g box pairs, and zero-g chains (outside the check) gain too. The pass keeps momentum to rounding (at most 7.1e-15 N s over 78 zero-g scenes); the gain is the solve's momentum drift, which shows once contacts stop absorbing energy.
- **Anchors (E4, 2026-11-05): analysis of alternatives.** Problem: a heavy striker drove a struck body into what it rests on, because the pass solved that body's supports in mass-split Jacobi rounds, and a light body between a heavy striker and the ground is an ill-conditioned chain whose rounds converge at about m_light / (m_heavy + m_light) per round. Measured without anchors (510 kg ball on a 1 kg cube, e 0.9): up/v 0.146 at 8 rounds, 0.143 at 32, 0.129 at 128, 0.049 at 512.
  - More rounds: rejected, the numbers above; each round costs 2 GPU dispatches.
  - Scale the pass back when it drives a body down (a bound): the pass is positively homogeneous, so scaling is exact and stops the drive, but the bounce stays dead (the ball keeps 0.14 of v). Rejected: it bounds the symptom and keeps the defect.
  - Mass-ratio-aware stiffness or a ramp, or substeps for high-ratio pairs: these act on the solve's penetration, not on the pass's conditioning, and the sink left after anchors (0.032 m at 510 kg, under the brief's 0.1 of the cube) does not need them. Not pursued.
  - Shock propagation (Guendelman 2003, freeze lower layers): the same idea without direction; it would freeze a body against whatever lies under it in the solve order, including what it does not sit on.
  - An exact LCP per island (Dantzig, Lemke): exact, but unbounded cost per island and branchy on the GPU.
  - Chosen, anchors: the loaded points of a struck body on the ground, or on a resting body it sits on (normal within 60° of up; the partner seated, not struck, and slower than `restitutionAnchorSpeed`, 0.25 m/s), are eliminated exactly: the body's mobility becomes M^-1/2 (1 - QQ^T) M^-1/2, Q an orthonormal basis of the anchor rows in M^1/2 coordinates (at most 6, Gram-Schmidt). That is direct elimination (a Schur complement), so it does not depend on the mass ratio; it is per body, so it maps onto the GPU's per-body kernels (one more dispatch, restAnchors, and a 288 B RestBody per body). Rejected inside it: letting go of the rows the free push would lift (gained 4x the strike's energy: a 1000 kg ball near a cube's edge); anchoring to the body above too (a plank struck from below was held down, and an S5 knock gained energy).
  - Reversibility: `restitutionAnchors: false` restores the pass as it was, to the float on the reference (pinned) and on SwiftShader (checked on 4 scenes against the GPU before the change). Restitution off is untouched (no buffer, pipeline or dispatch). Bounce stays opt-in, and level physics stay pinned by their hash. Known cost: anchors converge chains of simultaneous impacts further, so the pass's energy flaw there shows more often (Known limits; bounded by the energy cap since E4b).
  - A 1:1 box pair at 10 m/s ends at 1.023, 1.158 and 1.383x its kinetic energy at e 0.3, 0.6 and 0.9 (10 iterations). A 1:5 pair reaches 1.015x at e 0.9, and the 1:1 pair at e 0.9 reaches 1.061x at 20 iterations. With the switch off, these pairs' momentum already drifts by up to 46% at 10 iterations (15% at 20).

## GPU pair buffer

The GPU broadphase pairs bodies i < j whose bounding spheres overlap, |c_i - c_j| <= r_i + r_j + margin (0.02), each radius grown by the step's sweep, min(maxSweep = 1, dt (|v| + dt |g| + |w| r)), in world units. It writes at most `pairsPerBody` x capacity pairs a step; a step that needs more drops the pairs between the bodies latest in index order, which then pass through each other (`packages/physics-gpu/src/broadphase.ts`). A drop is never silent: every GPU test harness fails on one (#105), the bench and suite exit 4 and name the fix (#106), the viewer's HUD says so in red with a Rebuild button (#109), and the game's par lines on the GPU must hit none (`packages/game/test/par-gpu.ts`).

**Room per path.**

| Path | Room | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Engine default (bench, suite, probe, quality, GPU tests) | 16 (`DEFAULT_PAIRS_PER_BODY`) x (bodies + inputs) | holds every scenario at its defaults, below |
| Viewer | 16 x (bodies + inputs + 256 spare) | the spare is for fired projectiles |
| Viewer, S15 Kapla | 32 x capacity, box test on (`ScenarioDef.avbd`) | a collapsed 2k-plank pile needs 23-28 per plank by the sphere test, about half with the box test |
| Game | every pair of the level's bodies and rack (`everyPairPerBody`): L1 22, L2 71, L3 22, L4 20, L5 24 per body | its 0.5 m blocks reach past their 26 neighbours from about 3.3 m/s, inside the rack's speeds; a round that still drops pairs (a level past 362 bodies and rack) gives the level's later rounds the engine's fix (`roomAfter`) |

**Measured demand** (headless SwiftShader, 2026-10-28; pair counts, which are geometry, never timings; peak pairs per body at capacity = bodies, sphere test / box test):

| Case | Peak per body |
|---|---|
| S1 packed tower 8x8x30 at rest | 10.59 / 10.59 (the 26-neighbour lattice count, 20,336; `pair-room.test.ts`) |
| S4 city at rest (2k; 10k by a CPU count: 10.50) | 10.35 / 10.35 |
| S5 city 2k with its ball | 10.35 / 10.35 |
| S8 slab and rod tower | 10.38 / 3.63 |
| S14 buildings | 8.35 / 8.35 |
| S7 rain 2k; S6 heap 2k | 5.59 / 3.62; 4.19 / 2.55 |
| S11, S3, S12, S2, S13, S9 | 3.89 or less |
| S1 hit low by a 200 kg ball at 40 m/s; S5 2k hit at 80 m/s | 10.66; 11.12 |
| S3 wall hit by a size-5 ball at 80 m/s (the viewer's default scene) | 4.29 |

| Game par lines (L1-L5), against 16 per body | 233 of 704, 776 of 2272, 129 of 704, 98 of 656, 78 of 768 slots |

A resting unit-cube lattice pairs each cube with its 26 neighbours only, at most 13 per body. Moving packed blocks need more as the sweep grows: inside a block of unit cubes all moving faster than about 7.3 m/s each cube also reaches the cubes two apart (16 per body), past about 14.4 m/s the next shell (28), past about 20.8 m/s 40, and at the full sweep 125. The default grenade, the worst default case, needs 84% of the tightest room and is pinned by `packages/physics-gpu/test/pair-room.test.ts`.

**What can still drop, and how it shows.** In the viewer (HUD in red, with Rebuild): a grenade with stronger knobs in a packed block (a larger `radius` or `impulse`, or higher `maxDv` and `maxSpeed`: the default caps a blasted body at 48 m/s, a sweep of 0.8), a packed block falling faster than about 14 m/s, S8's Scene-panel variants (at rest, by a CPU count, bay 12 needs 20.6 per body and fits the viewer's spare by 4%; rodHeight 0.3 needs 18.2), and S15 with the box test turned off. In the bench and suite (exit 4): the same S8 variants at capacity = bodies, and S15 collapses on a plain `avbd-gpu` spec, since the suite builds engines from the spec given. In the game: a level of more than 362 bodies and rack no longer holds every pair (it keeps at least 65,536 slots, or 16 per body; at 10k bodies that is the default 16, and the game's 0.5 m blocks need more than 13 once a packed block moves at about 3.3 m/s). The game checks the engine's limits once each round is over (the round-over card, or a retry or load that ends it; `play.ts` `checkRound`): a round that dropped pairs shows a banner ("Physics limit hit: next round gets more room"), warns on the console, sets `window.__game.limitsHit`, and the level's later rounds are built with the engine's fix (`pairFix`: room for 1.25 x the peak, in multiples of 8, or the most the device holds). That round itself has already played wrong; a 10k level should get its room from measured demand when it is authored (roadmap G5). A level's physics block sets its room instead of every pair (`physics.pairsPerBody`, or the URL key `keystone.pairsPerBody=N`, which makes the level custom), for big levels and for tests and repros (`packages/game/test/pair-room-gpu.test.ts`).

**Deferred** (2026-10-28, the series stopped here): growing the buffer on overflow and replaying the step; the box test by default or automatically for thin bodies, which needs an M5 measurement first; a default scaled by capacity; S8's own viewer defaults; limits in bench:quality and a failing bench:probe; a limits notice in the viewer's `rules=keystone` HUD (the game's round banner shows there too); M5 measurements of S15 pile demand at 10k and 100k planks.

## Rest stability at the game's settings

Roadmap F3: big structures left idle, and struck then left, must stay where they came to rest at the game's solver settings (`packages/physics-gpu/test/rest-stability.ts`). Structures: the S3 wall (20 courses), the S4 city (S5's ball when struck), and S15 Kapla at real plank size (its own box test and 32 pair slots, real gravity, autoSubsteps engine steps a frame, as the viewer runs it). Idle: the window runs from frame 120 to the end; struck: a ball at about 1 s (S15: its own topple), and the window is the run's second half. The verdict: over the window no structure body moved more than a tenth of the thinnest body's thickness (0.1 for cubes, 1/30 of a width for planks), the top stayed put to that much, and no engine limit was hit. CI runs a 400-cube wall idle (600 frames) and a 200-cube wall struck (1,200 frames); `PHYSICS_GPU_SLOW=1` runs the matrix at `REST_SIZES` for `REST_FRAMES` (3,600, a minute), and `TB_CHANNEL=chrome` runs it on the real GPU. It was structures x idle, struck x 20, 40 iterations x gapSlop on, off; since #310 it gates only what the game claims holds (below).
